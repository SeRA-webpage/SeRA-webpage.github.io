Old Experience, New Lessons

Relabeling Autonomous Robot Rollouts in Interactive Imitation Learning

Anonymous Authors

Under review

Method Interactive Demo Videos Results

TL;DR: SeRA relabels old robot experience with later policies, then learns from the relabels as desired action sets, not exact targets.

Autonomous evaluation rollouts at 4× speed. The inset is the wrist camera.

On the real robot, SeRA succeeds in 16 of 20 trials, compared with 8 of 20 for SDP, using the same human feedback and training budget.

Abstract: Interactive imitation learning enables robots to learn from human interventions during deployment. These interactions naturally produce sparse human corrections and abundant autonomous robot experience without human action labels. Yet existing methods either leave this experience unused for action supervision or imitate historical robot actions, which can reinforce suboptimal behavior. To address this limitation, we propose Set-valued Relabeling of Autonomous experience (SeRA), a framework that learns from autonomous robot experience through set-valued supervision of policy relabels. Later policy checkpoints relabel this experience, and agreement across checkpoints filters the relabels. Since agreement does not guarantee correctness, SeRA constructs desired action sets around accepted relabels. Following Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (SDP), it trains an action-chunking diffusion policy to increase the probability of actions within these sets. A step-masked denoising loss combines accepted relabels with available human corrections in partially supervised chunks. Experiments across five simulated manipulation tasks in online and offline settings show that SeRA improves policy performance over SDP and outperforms baselines that imitate historical robot actions or exact relabels. On a real-robot tomato-picking task, SeRA achieves 80% success, compared with 40% for SDP, using the same human feedback and training budget. These results demonstrate that relabeled autonomous robot experience can complement human corrections to improve policy performance.

Human corrections are only part of the data

Most interaction data has no human label. In interactive imitation learning, a human teacher intervenes during deployment. This produces sparse human corrections and abundant autonomous robot experience without human action labels. Learning only from the corrections leaves this experience unused for action supervision.

Historical robot actions can be suboptimal. Prior work retains them as imitation targets: IWR imitates them alongside the corrections, CEILing selects useful behavior with evaluative feedback, and Sirius downweights pre-intervention actions. However, an autonomous action need not be optimal, even when it does not trigger an intervention or occurs in a successful trajectory. Retaining it as a target can reinforce suboptimal behavior.

Later policies can teach earlier observations

Key insight. Later policies incorporate additional human corrections and may generalize these corrections to observations collected earlier. SeRA therefore treats autonomous robot experience as unlabeled data and revisits it with later policies.

Open the complete figure

SeRA has three components:

  • Cross-round policy relabeling. Later checkpoints relabel earlier observations, and agreement across checkpoints filters the relabels.
  • Set-valued targets. Each accepted relabel defines a desired action set. Following SDP, the action-chunking diffusion policy is trained to increase the probability of actions within these sets, so it need not reproduce an imperfect relabel exactly.
  • Step-masked denoising loss. Accepted relabels and available human corrections are combined within one chunk, and unsupported steps are excluded from the loss.

Agreement can be imperfect, so learn from sets

Multiple checkpoints can share the same error, so agreement is a proxy for reliability, not a guarantee. SeRA uses each accepted relabel as the center of a desired action set instead of an exact target. The radius is scaled by the distance to the historical robot action. Experiments

We evaluate SeRA in online and offline learning across five simulated manipulation tasks, and on a real-robot tomato-picking task. All baselines are built on SDP and differ in how they use autonomous robot experience.

Simulation Benchmarks

Five tasks: PushT, PickCan, Square, TwoArmLift, and ThreePiece. Online, each method collects its own data with a simulated teacher. Offline, all methods train on the same dataset. The methods are:

  • SDP learns only from complete teacher-labeled chunks.
  • IWR imitates all autonomous robot experience, and CEILing imitates robot actions from successful trajectories.
  • Sirius assigns zero weight to pre-intervention actions.
  • Pseudo-labeling uses the same relabeling and filtering as SeRA, but imitates accepted relabels as exact targets.
  • StepMask is the variant of SeRA without relabeling.

Offline success rate (%), mean ± standard deviation over three seeds. “w/o filtering” removes checkpoint consistency filtering. “w/o set-valued targets” sets the relabel radius ratio to 10−4, yielding approximately point-valued supervision. “w/o relabeling” uses only retained human supervision (StepMask).

  • Consistency filtering improves learning from relabels. Removing it lowers success from 96.3% to 31.4% on Square and from 98.9% to 9.5% on ThreePiece.
  • Set-valued targets improve task success. With filtering retained, approximately point-valued targets lower success on all four tasks, most sharply on Square and ThreePiece.
  • Relabeling complements partial human supervision. It improves success on all four tasks over the variant that uses only retained human supervision.
